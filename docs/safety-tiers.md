## Three risk tiers (spec 8.3)

Every registry entry declares one tier; the tier decides what the AI may do, who signs and how it is explained to a regulator. The sign-off roles are the union of the registry entries' `required_signoff_role`.

| Tier | Definition | Examples (spec) | AI may | Signs or approves: spec; registry | Regulation and validation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `ops` Operations | Does not touch individual clinical decisions; affects operations and scheduling only. | Control Tower, call centre, supplies and staffing | predict, narrate, recommend actions | operations manager, charge nurse, clerk; registry: clerk, medical director, nurse, operations manager | Privacy (PHIPA) and audit are enough; no medical-device question. |
| `documentation` Documentation | Drafts clinical documents from content already in the record. | Discharge summary, patient instructions, nursing handoff, follow-up record | summarise, draft, rewrite in plain language, translate | physician, nurse; registry: clerk, medical director, nurse, physician, radiologist | The physician's signature is the hard gate and the traceability validator the technical gate; a draft is not the record. |
| `clinical_ds` Clinical decision support | Gives judgement-type suggestions on diagnosis or treatment. | Order review, medication-reconciliation discrepancies, critical values and alerts, imaging report drafts | flag, rank, draft; never block, execute | pharmacist, physician; registry: medical director, nurse, pharmacist, physician, radiologist, technologist | Health Canada's software as a medical device (SaMD) territory. A general-purpose model has no clinical validation; the demo proves the pipeline and claims no clinical performance. |

## Guardrails

| Guardrail | Rule | Spec | Where it is enforced | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PHI minimisation | Everything sent to a model is de-identified first (structured fields, FHIR resources, free text with a model second pass); patients appear only as a hash of their MRN. | 6.3 | `apps/api/app/llm/freetext_deid.py`<br>`apps/api/app/llm/fhir_deid.py`<br>`apps/api/app/agents/runtime.py` | evals/deid (recall >= 0.98, precision >= 0.90) |
| Prompt-injection defence and privilege blocking | Patient and outside text sits in an untrusted block it cannot close; the Tool Gateway authorises from the registry and the caller's identity only, never from what a model claims. | 6.4 | `apps/api/app/agents/untrusted.py`<br>`apps/api/app/agents/gateway.py` | evals/injection (attack calls executed: 0) |

## Honest boundaries (said unprompted when asked)

- All data is synthetic: the hospital comes from this project's own Synthea-style generator and the imaging centre from its seeded generator. There is no real patient anywhere.
- Model metrics show that the pipeline works, not clinical performance. A real deployment would retrain on the hospital's own history, validate retrospectively, then run prospectively in shadow mode.
- Every committed eval result is a mock-model baseline; runs on a real model (Claude or Gemini) and the human rating sheets are still to be done.
- The pharmacy rule tables are simplified demo versions; production needs a commercial drug knowledge base.
- There is no real HL7 interface engine: ADT, order and result messages come from the day simulator. A real system replaces only the event source.

## Definition of done

Definition of done (the supplement's ten items, merged into each section's acceptance criteria): every module ships code, unit tests, an eval set with a threshold, demo data and a demo script; it is registered with a risk tier and a signer before it may write.
