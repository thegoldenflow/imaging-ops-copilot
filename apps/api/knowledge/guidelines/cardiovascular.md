## hyperkalaemia
title: Drugs that raise potassium
keywords: lisinopril, ace inhibitor, spironolactone, potassium, potassium chloride, hyperkalaemia, renal, egfr, heart failure
An ACE inhibitor, spironolactone and potassium supplements each raise serum potassium; together, or with kidney disease, they can cause dangerous hyperkalaemia. Check potassium and creatinine within days of a change, and avoid routine potassium supplements with spironolactone.

## statins
title: Statin duplication and interactions
keywords: atorvastatin, simvastatin, statin, duplicate, clarithromycin, amiodarone, myopathy, rhabdomyolysis, hyperlipidemia, myocardial infarction, stroke
Two statins together add myopathy risk without benefit; keep one at the intended intensity. Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors such as clarithromycin are contraindicated with simvastatin; with amiodarone, simvastatin is limited to 20 mg a day.

## rhythm-drugs
title: Amiodarone and digoxin
keywords: amiodarone, digoxin, warfarin, atrial fibrillation, interaction, levels, toxicity, qt
Amiodarone roughly doubles digoxin levels (halve the digoxin dose and check a level) and raises the INR on warfarin over several weeks. Digoxin above 0.125 mg a day in older adults adds toxicity risk.

## rate-control-continuity
title: Continuing rate-control and antihypertensive drugs
keywords: metoprolol, beta blocker, amlodipine, lisinopril, hydrochlorothiazide, antihypertensive, omission, blood pressure, atrial fibrillation, hypertension
Stopping a beta blocker abruptly can cause rebound tachycardia and ischaemia; an omission at admission should be deliberate (for example low blood pressure or sepsis) and documented. Antihypertensives left off at admission are a common unintended discrepancy; resume or document why they are held.
