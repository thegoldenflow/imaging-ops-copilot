## sedatives-falls
title: Sedatives, opioids and falls
keywords: lorazepam, benzodiazepine, quetiapine, antipsychotic, morphine, hydromorphone, tramadol, opioid, gabapentin, falls, delirium, sedation, older, elderly, fracture
Benzodiazepines, antipsychotics and sedating drugs raise the risk of delirium, falls and fractures in older adults. Avoid starting them for sleep or agitation where possible; when needed, use the lowest dose for the shortest time. An opioid with a benzodiazepine adds respiratory depression.

## nsaids-older
title: NSAIDs in older adults
keywords: nsaid, ibuprofen, naproxen, ketorolac, celecoxib, older, elderly, gastrointestinal, bleeding, kidney, heart failure
In older adults NSAIDs carry more gastrointestinal bleeding, kidney injury and heart-failure risk. Avoid long-term use, especially with an anticoagulant or corticosteroid; if used, short courses at the lowest dose with gastric protection. Ketorolac is limited to 15 mg a dose and 60 mg a day from 65.

## start-low
title: Start low, go slow
keywords: older, elderly, age, dose, digoxin, lorazepam, tramadol, acetaminophen, frail, low weight
Older and low-weight patients need smaller doses of many drugs: digoxin above 0.125 mg a day adds toxicity without benefit, tramadol is capped at 300 mg a day over 75, acetaminophen is capped by weight under 50 kg. Review every new drug for a lower starting dose.
