## egfr-basics
title: Using eGFR for drug decisions
keywords: egfr, creatinine, creatinine clearance, renal, kidney, ckd, dose adjustment, monitoring
Most labelled renal adjustments are written for creatinine clearance; eGFR is a reasonable stand-in for stable kidney function in adults of average size. An eGFR older than a few months, or a rising creatinine during the stay, should be rechecked before relying on it.

## nsaids-ckd
title: NSAIDs in chronic kidney disease
keywords: nsaid, ibuprofen, naproxen, ketorolac, celecoxib, renal, egfr, acute kidney injury, ckd, lisinopril, diuretic, furosemide, hydrochlorothiazide
NSAIDs reduce renal blood flow and can cause acute kidney injury, especially with an ACE inhibitor and a diuretic together. Avoid them when eGFR is below 30 mL/min/1.73 m2 and use acetaminophen for pain; ketorolac is contraindicated in advanced renal impairment.

## metformin-sitagliptin
title: Glucose-lowering drugs and kidney function
keywords: metformin, sitagliptin, glyburide, diabetes, renal, egfr, lactic acidosis, hypoglycaemia
Metformin is contraindicated below an eGFR of 30 mL/min/1.73 m2 (lactic acidosis) and is not started between 30 and 45. Sitagliptin is reduced to 25 mg daily below 30. Glyburide accumulates and causes prolonged hypoglycaemia in kidney disease.

## gabapentinoids-gout
title: Gabapentin, allopurinol, colchicine and spironolactone
keywords: gabapentin, pregabalin, allopurinol, colchicine, spironolactone, hyperkalaemia, potassium, renal, egfr, sedation, falls
Gabapentin accumulates and causes sedation and falls: 200-700 mg a day at a clearance of 15-29 mL/min. Allopurinol starts low (100 mg daily or less) and is titrated to urate. Colchicine prophylaxis starts at 0.3 mg a day below 30 mL/min. Spironolactone is avoided below an eGFR of 30 because of hyperkalaemia.

## antimicrobials-renal
title: Antimicrobials that need renal adjustment
keywords: nitrofurantoin, levofloxacin, ciprofloxacin, sulfamethoxazole, vancomycin, piperacillin, renal, egfr, antibiotic
Nitrofurantoin is avoided at a clearance below 30 mL/min. Levofloxacin, ciprofloxacin, sulfamethoxazole-trimethoprim and piperacillin need longer intervals or smaller doses in severe impairment. Vancomycin is dosed by weight and adjusted by levels.
